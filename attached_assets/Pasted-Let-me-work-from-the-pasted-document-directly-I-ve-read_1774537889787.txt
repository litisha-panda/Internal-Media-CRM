## Hard Audit — Developer + Product Manager Perspective

### The Core Question: Is It Solving the Revenue Loss Problem?

No. Not yet. The tool has the right UI skin on a broken engine. Here's why.

**The revenue loss at OTV isn't because reps aren't meeting clients.** It's because deals enter a pipeline and silently die there. No one chases them. No one escalates them. No one even knows they've gone cold until the quarter ends. This tool as built doesn't fix that loop — it just displays the problem more attractively.

---

### The Logical Flow That Must Exist (and Largely Doesn't)

A deal has exactly 5 stages that must be enforced, not just displayed:

**Stage 1 → Deal created with approved target.** Partial. The target approval chain (Rep → RH → NSH → Strategy → CRO) exists in the UI. But it's not enforced. A rep can add a deal with a ₹0 target and it sits in the pipeline as a valid record. There's no gate.

**Stage 2 → Meeting logged daily.** Partial. The 11:30 PM deadline logic exists and absence fires correctly. But there's zero push. No WhatsApp, no email, no in-app notification to the rep at 9 PM saying "you haven't logged today." The only way anyone knows is by logging into the tool and checking. Reps won't do that.

**Stage 3 → Next step with owner and date.** Broken. This is the single biggest revenue leak point. When a rep logs a meeting and sets a next step, the tool records it. But when that date passes with no action, nothing happens. No alert. No escalation. No flag to the manager. The deal just sits there with a red "overdue" status that nobody will see unless they specifically go to War Room. This is where deals go to die.

**Stage 4 → Approval chain resolves.** Broken. This is technically built but logically wrong. When the NSH clicks "Approve" on a deal, the code sets `awaitingApproval = null`. The flag is cleared. The deal moves nowhere. There is no chain. If Rohit raises a discount approval request and GK (NSH) approves it, the deal doesn't update to "approved — proceed." It just stops being blocked. No audit trail of who approved what. No notification to Rohit that it's cleared. No push to the next level if GK doesn't act in 48 hours.

**Stage 5 → Revenue captured against target.** Disconnected. The Revenue Log tab exists. Deals exist. Targets exist. None of these three data sources talk to each other automatically. A rep can close a ₹50L deal, log it as "Proposal Accepted," and the Revenue Log shows zero for that client unless someone manually creates a revenue entry. The "Closed QTD" KPI card in the pipeline is reading `outcome === "Proposal Accepted"` — it's counting stage labels, not actual booked revenue. That's a vanity metric, not a financial reality.

### Is Everything Connected From an Admin Perspective?

Structurally, no. These are the disconnections that matter:

**The two meeting log paths don't sync.** The inline "Tap to log" form in My Plan reads DOM element IDs using `document.getElementById()` — it's completely decoupled from the `logForm` state in `BLANK_LOG`. A rep who logs via My Plan bypasses the structured next-step items, the internal request creation, and the calendar integration. Their meeting still gets recorded, but none of the downstream automation triggers.

**CROApp doesn't write back to the parent's persisted state.** `deals` and `meetings` inside CROApp are local `useState`. The `usePersistedState` hook is at the parent level. When a manager updates a deal outcome in the pipeline view, that change lives only in CROApp's local state and won't survive a page refresh. This is the most dangerous data bug in the current build.

**RepDailyView is dead code.** ~400 lines of a purpose-built rep interface that is never rendered. Reps load the full CROApp instead. That means a Sales Rep logging in sees the same codebase as the CRO — just with certain tabs hidden. The performance cost is real: the entire app loads including RO parser, all seed data, all management views.

**The absence system is server-side logic running client-side.** The EOD check that fires absence reports exists as `runEODCheck()` — a function that runs when the Admin manually clicks "Simulate EOD Run." In production this must be a cron job on the server at 23:30 every night. That infrastructure doesn't exist. Right now the entire attendance system is theatre.

**No notifications anywhere.** The tool generates tasks, flags deals, marks absences — but sends nothing. No email to the rep when a task is assigned. No WhatsApp to the NSH when an approval breaches 48h SLA. No alert to the CXO when a Top 5 deal goes 14 days without contact. All the right data is there. None of it is pushed. A tool that requires you to log in to discover you have a problem isn't a management system — it's a reporting dashboard.

### What's Missing From a Usability Standpoint

**For Sales Reps (the daily users):** No mobile layout. This is the primary device for field reps. The compliance system requires daily logging and tomorrow's planning — both of which a rep would do on their phone, often in transit. The current layout is desktop-only and will break below 768px.

**For NSH/CRO (the decision makers):** No single number that answers "what is my revenue at risk today?" The War Room comes closest but requires you to read 5 different sections to assemble that answer. A $-figure "Revenue at Risk" metric — combining blocked deals + overdue next steps + cold accounts — doesn't exist anywhere.

**For Admin:** No way to verify data integrity. Which reps have deals with no next step set? Which deals have been in the same stage for 30+ days? Which meetings were logged but have no discussion text? There's no data quality view, no completeness audit, no "incomplete records" report. An admin cannot currently tell if the data in the system is usable or garbage.

**For Everyone:** No search. You cannot search for a client name, deal, contact, or meeting note anywhere in the tool. With 12+ deals seeded and real usage generating hundreds more, this becomes unusable within weeks.

### Why This Matters for Revenue — Performance Perspective Only

OTV's revenue problem is a velocity problem. Deals don't close because they stall. They stall because no one is accountable for moving them. The tool as currently built tracks stalling but doesn't prevent it or force resolution. That's the gap.

The specific scenario that repeats: Vikram has ₹3.6Cr of national deals. Three of them are "awaiting approval." The approval was requested 7 days ago. GK (NSH) hasn't seen it because he has to log in and navigate to Escalations to find it. He doesn't do that every day. Those three deals are effectively frozen. The tool shows the freeze. It does not break it.

The three things that would immediately move revenue:

First, auto-escalation with a push. When a deal's next step date passes with no log entry against it, the rep's manager gets a WhatsApp/email that names the deal, the amount, and the days idle. Not a dashboard to check — a message that requires acknowledgment.

Second, approval SLA enforcement with chain push. If NSH doesn't act on an approval in 48 hours, it auto-escalates to CXO with a flag. The current "awaiting approval" SLA badge is cosmetic. Make it functional — add an Approve/Reject button that logs the action, notifies the rep, and pushes the deal to the next required step.

Third, link Revenue Log to deal close. When a deal is moved to "Proposal Accepted," auto-create a revenue entry stub with the deal amount, the rep, the quarter, and mark it "PO Pending." The rep confirms the PO amount when received. Now the Revenue Log reflects actual bookings, not pipeline labels.

### What to Build Next — In Exact Priority Order

1. Fix the state sync bug — `setSharedDeals` and `setSharedMeetings` must be called inside CROApp on every mutation. This is a one-line fix per setter, ~20 places.

2. Fix the approval chain — "Approve" must set `awaitingApproval` to the next person in chain, not null. Add Approve/Reject with reason field. Log the action with timestamp and actor.

3. Auto-escalation logic — a `useEffect` that runs on load and checks every deal where `nextStepDate < today` and no meeting logged since `nextStepDate`. Flag these deals with an "ESCALATED" state and surface them prominently in every manager view.

4. Revenue entry auto-stub on deal close — when `outcome` flips to "Proposal Accepted," create a `revenueEntries` record. Link deal ID to revenue entry. Now targets, pipeline, and revenue are one connected system.

5. Push notifications foundation — even if it's just an email to `hr@odishatv.com` on absence, the infrastructure needs to exist. Use a simple `fetch()` to a webhook (Zapier/Make) that routes to WhatsApp or email. This can be wired in a day.

6. Global search — a single input in the topbar that filters across deals, clients, meetings, and tasks. One `useMemo` over all state arrays, filtered by a search string. Two hours of work, massive daily usability gain.

7. Mobile layout for My Plan and Log Meeting only. These are the two views a rep uses in the field. They don't need the full responsive treatment — just a media query breakpoint that stacks the two-column layouts to single column below 640px.

Until points 1–4 are done, the tool is a better-looking version of a spreadsheet. After points 1–4, it starts to close the loop that currently causes revenue to leak.
